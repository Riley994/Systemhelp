03 THE METHOD

The six pillars of a high Team IQ

High Team IQ is not a personality type or an away-day result. It is a set of team conditions that can be measured, practised and improved against real work.

The system

Six pillars make the team conditions visible. A five-stage measurement cycle turns those conditions into working evidence.

  • Six pillars and twenty-two chapters.
  • Five measurement stages.
  • Fourteen named tools.

High Team IQ is a system, not a slogan

A high-performing team is not simply a group of high-performing individuals. It is a group that can create better outcomes together than its members could create alone. That requires more than technical skill. The team must see the same valuable target, exchange useful information, own outcomes, learn before risk becomes rework, make timely decisions and judge quality by value as well as output.

The six TEAM IQ pillars provide a practical map for those conditions: TARGET, EXPRESSION, ACCOUNTABILITY, MINDSET, INSIGHT and QUALITY. Each pillar has its own chapters, but they work as one system. A clear target without open expression becomes a plan that nobody challenges. Expression without accountability becomes a good conversation with no dependable follow-through. Insight without quality can favour a quick local win over customer value and lifecycle cost.

TARGET: make the result visible

TARGET is the team’s shared line of sight to a valuable result. It is not a broad ambition or a task list. A useful target is specific enough to measure, clear enough to explain and meaningful enough to guide trade-offs. The team should be able to name the baseline, the intended change, the review date and the reason the work matters.

In its four chapters it covers TRUE, ACHIEVABLE, REQUIRED, GIVE, EXPLAINABLE and TRACEABLE. They ask whether the destination and urgency are real, whether the work deserves capacity, whether achievement is recognised through observable contribution, and whether activity can be traced to stakeholder value. The pillar also makes risk part of the target rather than an afterthought.

The result is a team that can explain what winning looks like, stop work that lacks value and expose the assumptions most likely to prevent success. It gives every later conversation a common reference point.

EXPRESSION: make information usable

EXPRESSION is the ability to exchange ideas, concerns, disagreement, uncertainty and learning in a safe, clear and useful way. It is not the volume of messages or the number of meetings. The test is whether the right people understand enough to improve the work.

The four chapters cover create safety and lead by example; expectations, the Clarity Code and the 5 C’s; active listening through E.A.R.S and regular team building; and L.E.A.N conflict resolution with Yin Yang feedback. Together they make it easier to challenge a claim without attacking a person, listen before replying and turn feedback into a change in value, risk, quality or learning.

This pillar matters when a warning is still small. A team that can say “this is an assumption” or “I may be missing something” has more information available before a decision becomes expensive. It does not avoid conflict. It makes conflict productive.

ACCOUNTABILITY: own an outcome, not a task

ACCOUNTABILITY is shared certainty about who owns an outcome, what evidence will prove progress and what a usable handoff looks like. It includes individual follow-through and team responsibility. If a condition changes, the team does not hide behind a role chart. It shows the evidence, shares the learning and acts on the result.

Its two chapters are define roles and model accountability, and regular check-ins, boundaries and agreements. The first moves commitments from activities to measurable outcomes. The second makes the edges between roles explicit: one primary owner, named contributors and conditions a receiving role can use.

Strong accountability is not blame. It is the ability to ask, “What did we expect to change? What happened? Who owns the next move? What support or boundary is needed?” That creates dependable delivery without pretending uncertainty does not exist.

MINDSET: learn faster than the risk grows

MINDSET is the team’s shared way of interpreting challenge, uncertainty, failure, difference and opportunity. Under pressure, teams can defend the plan, hide a problem and keep work within familiar silos. A stronger mindset treats difficulty as information. It asks what the team can test cheaply and early.

Its three chapters are make a growth mindset and innovate; networking and distributed shared ownership; and synergy, education and team tributes. The pillar makes learning a delivery practice. It frames an initiative as a hypothesis, brings different functions into the work before the formal handoff, and recognises contributions that changed an outcome rather than simply visible effort.

A team with this pillar does not celebrate failed work. It values early evidence that prevents larger waste. It tests the highest consequence uncertainty first, changes direction when evidence requires it and makes cross-functional knowledge available before it is too late to use.

INSIGHT: decide where information is richest

INSIGHT is the team’s ability to make sound, timely, evidence-aware decisions at the point where information is richest. It combines decentralised authority with shared purpose, connected measures, feedback loops, clear channels and transparent information. The aim is not local freedom without limits. It is accountable judgement within a visible boundary.

Its three chapters are decentralised decision making and purpose-based leadership; interconnected metrics and regular feedback loops; and clear channels and transparent information sharing. A leader states the outcome and guardrails. The team can act within them, record its reasoning and escalate when the decision crosses the agreed threshold.

This avoids a common delivery cost: decisions that wait in a hierarchy queue while the most relevant evidence is already near the work. Good insight makes the route and review date clear. It leaves an audit trail without making people wait for permission to think.

QUALITY: deliver the right value at the right cost

QUALITY is the ability to deliver the right value, to the right standard, at the right cost and at the right time. It is not perfection for its own sake. A technically polished feature can still be a quality failure if customers do not value it, if it creates avoidable support cost or if it delays a more important outcome.

Its three chapters are continue with the end in mind and value stream mapping; customer-centric design and continuous feedback; and corrective QA, cost analysis and cross-functional collaboration. The pillar sets “good enough” standards and guardrails before work begins, looks for waiting and rework across the flow, and judges quality using customer, business and technical evidence together.

QUALITY is where a team asks whether it should continue, stop or change a piece of work once the required value has been reached. It prevents gold-plating and makes lifecycle cost visible before a short-term decision becomes a long-term burden.

The five-stage measurement cycle

The pillars describe what a high-Team-IQ team needs to be able to do. The measurement cycle shows how to run that work in a live delivery context. It has five stages: Set, Communicate, Own, Learn, Decide and improve. The cycle is not a separate reporting layer. Its tools make intention, evidence, ownership, learning, decisions and outcomes visible.

Set

Set asks, “What valuable change are we aiming to create?” The T.A.R.G.E.T Value Index (TVI) records the outcome, baseline, target and review date. The Value-to-Effort Ratio (VER) makes trade-offs explicit by comparing estimated value impact with cost or effort. Target Risk Burn-Down (TRB) identifies the assumptions, dependencies and obstacles most likely to block the target, then tracks whether exposure is reducing.

Communicate

Communicate asks, “What do we know, what are we assuming, and what has changed?” The C.L.E.A.R.L.Y Evidence Standard (CES) distinguishes evidence, assumption, hypothesis and test plan. The Expression of Value Delta (EVD) moves updates away from activity and toward observable change: what value, risk, quality or learning moved because of the work?

Own

Own asks, “Who owns this outcome, and what evidence will prove it?” The D.R.E.A.M Outcome Contract (DOC) names the expected outcome, measure, evidence and review. Quantified D.R.E.A.M Boundaries (QDB) clarifies primary ownership, contributors and measurable handoff conditions. The team can then distinguish a completed handoff from a message merely sent.

Learn

Learn asks, “What are we testing, and what uncertainty are we reducing?” M.I.N.D.S.E.T Value Engineering (MVE) frames a testable statement: if the team takes an action, a defined measure should move from a baseline to a target within a timeframe. Risk-First Learning Loops (RLL) directs experiments to the highest consequence risks before low-risk feature polish.

Decide and improve

Decide and improve asks, “What should we continue, change, stop or scale?” D.I.R.E.C.T Value Routing (DVR) uses value, evidence and confidence to choose the next move. Insight Risk Triggers (IRT) set thresholds for pausing, pivoting, escalating or testing again. D.I.R.E.C.T Decision Audits (DDA) review how well a decision was made. The 7 C’s Value Engineering Standard (CVE) defines the required quality threshold, while 7 C’s Outcome Metrics (COM) connects quality to customer, business and technical evidence.

Use the cycle to improve the weakest constraint

The aim is not to apply every tool at once. Start where the team has its greatest constraint. A team with a vague target should not begin with an elaborate decision audit. A team with late risk discovery may need a risk-first learning loop before it needs another reporting meeting. Build evidence of improvement, then use that evidence to choose the next move.

This is the practical meaning of raising Team IQ. The team becomes more capable of creating the right response when the plan, process or individual expert is not enough. It can see, communicate, own, learn, decide and improve together.
