What this report is

This is a redacted sample of the evidence-led output provided after a TEAM IQ diagnostic session. It combines participant input, working artefacts and observed operating patterns; it is not an appraisal of any named individual. The client in this example is deliberately identified only as Client A.

What we measured

TARGET

A shared, measurable and traceable outcome with meaningful value.

EXPRESSION

Safe, clear, balanced communication and evidence-led challenge.

ACCOUNTABILITY

Visible ownership, outcome contracts and measurable handoffs.

MINDSET

Learning, experimentation and shared ownership under uncertainty.

INSIGHT

Decision routing, connected measures and transparent information.

QUALITY

End-to-end value, customer evidence and prevention over rework.

Method note: scores are directional indicators used to prioritise a conversation and a practical next cycle, not labels for people or teams.

Where the loss is concentrated

Weakest · EXPRESSION 47

Challenge arrived late or privately

Across the anonymous evidence set, three of six teams had no agreed Clarity Code for where a material concern, decision or action should be recorded. In review conversations, quieter contributors reported waiting for a separate conversation before raising a risk.

Pattern: evidence was often available, but did not reach the decision point in time.

Second weakest · INSIGHT 52

Decision ownership was hard to follow

For sampled cross-team decisions, the observed elapsed time from issue raised to recorded decision averaged nine days. Decision context, owner and review date were frequently split across meeting notes and chat.

Pattern: teams could discuss an issue, but could not reliably route, record and revisit the decision.

Interpretation: this sample does not show a capability deficit in individuals. It shows a system problem: information, challenge and decisions need a shared route so the teams can use their existing expertise sooner.

Prioritised 90-day plan

Build visible habits in the two weakest pillars, then verify whether daily work is changing.

WhenFocusSpecific TEAM IQ tools appliedPractical output
Days 1–30Make priority and challenge visibleT.A.R.G.E.T Value Index (TVI); Clarity Code Pulse; C.L.E.A.R.L.Y Evidence Standard (CES)One TVI card per team; agreed channels and a two-week sample of material claims.
Days 31–60Route decisions and ownershipD.I.R.E.C.T Value Routing (DVR); D.R.E.A.M Outcome Contracts (DOC); Quantified D.R.E.A.M Boundaries (QDB)Shared decision log; named outcome owners; measurable handoff conditions for priority work.
Days 61–90Learn, adapt and embedRisk-First Learning Loops (RLL); Expression of Value Delta (EVD); D.I.R.E.C.T Decision Audits (DDA)Evidence-led experiments, value updates and a review of decision quality and speed.

How we will prove it worked

  • Re-run the six-pillar diagnostic at day 90 and compare pillar movement, not only the overall score.
  • Measure the share of members who can accurately explain each priority TVI without looking at it.
  • Sample material claims against CES and track the percentage with evidence, an assumption or a defined test.
  • Measure issue-raised-to-recorded-decision time and audit whether owner, evidence and review date are present.
  • Track first-time QDB handoffs and the quality, timing or ownership cause where a handoff fails.

Risk of doing nothing

When a team has no common route for targets, challenge and decisions, the cost is usually hidden before it is financial: late escalation, repeated discussion, avoidable rework and growing reliance on a few people to translate the system. Client A’s sample pattern is unlikely to correct itself without a shared practice.
