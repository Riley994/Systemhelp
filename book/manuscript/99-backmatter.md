## The fourteen instruments

An instrument in TEAM IQ is a working artefact: a card, board, log, threshold, contract or review that gives the team evidence it can use. It is not a separate reporting layer. Each instrument belongs in the work itself, where a target is set, a claim is tested, a handoff is agreed or a decision is made. The point is to replace meetings that depend on memory, confidence or status colour with evidence that the people doing the work can inspect.

| Cycle stage | The question the stage answers | Instruments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set | What valuable change are we aiming to create? | T.A.R.G.E.T Value Index (TVI); Value-to-Effort Ratio (VER); Target Risk Burn-Down (TRB) |
| Communicate | What do we know, what are we assuming, and what has changed? | C.L.E.A.R.L.Y Evidence Standard (CES); Expression of Value Delta (EVD) |
| Own | Who owns this outcome, and what evidence will prove it? | D.R.E.A.M Outcome Contract (DOC); Quantified D.R.E.A.M Boundaries (QDB) |
| Learn | What are we testing, and what uncertainty are we reducing? | M.I.N.D.S.E.T Value Engineering (MVE); Risk-First Learning Loops (RLL) |
| Decide and improve | What should we continue, change, stop, or scale? | D.I.R.E.C.T Value Routing (DVR); Insight Risk Triggers (IRT); D.I.R.E.C.T Decision Audits (DDA); 7 C's Value Engineering Standard (CVE); 7 C's Outcome Metrics (COM) |

**T.A.R.G.E.T Value Index (TVI).** The TVI produces a card containing the stakeholder value to change, its scale, the current baseline, the required movement, a guardrail, an owner and a review rhythm. Use it when the team first sets a material outcome and read it again whenever work is prioritised or reviewed. It replaces a broad ambition, a delivery date without a value measure, and a plan that mistakes completed activity for success.

**Value-to-Effort Ratio (VER).** VER produces a comparable view of estimated value impact divided by estimated cost or effort, with confidence recorded alongside the estimate. Use it to rank the next cycle's options and again when evidence changes the estimate. It replaces a backlog ordered by volume, seniority, habit or the last person to make the case.

**Target Risk Burn-Down (TRB).** TRB produces a visible, prioritised risk view: an uncertain event, its impact and probability, its exposure score, the smallest reduction experiment and the evidence from review. Use it before commitment, maintain it weekly for an active initiative, and bring the highest exposures into planning. It replaces a risk register that is completed for governance and then left behind until a problem becomes expensive.

**C.L.E.A.R.L.Y Evidence Standard (CES).** CES produces an evidence log in which a significant claim is identified as evidence, an assumption or a hypothesis with a test plan; the later learning is recorded as well. Use it in planning, review and decision meetings, especially where a confident assertion could harden into a commitment. It replaces untested opinion presented as fact and makes challenge less personal because the team can inspect the claim rather than judge the speaker.

**Expression of Value Delta (EVD).** EVD produces an update that states what changed, which value, quality, risk or operational measure should move, the before and current state, the learning, and the next action. Use it in stand-ups, delivery reviews and retrospectives after a change or test. It replaces a percentage-complete report or an activity update that cannot say what changed for a customer, the organisation or the team's understanding.

**D.R.E.A.M Outcome Contract (DOC).** A DOC produces a short outcome commitment: the intended change, relevant measure, baseline and intended movement, evidence, timeframe, dependencies and the learning rule if the result does not occur. Use it when major work is committed for a cycle and review it at the agreed date. It replaces an assignment phrased only as a task and a review that asks whether someone was busy rather than whether the expected result appeared.

**Quantified D.R.E.A.M Boundaries (QDB).** QDB produces an ownership board that assigns one primary owner and named contributors to each material outcome, measure or risk, with decision boundaries and measurable handoff conditions. Use it when work crosses roles, when a handoff has failed, and at regular ownership reviews. It replaces implied responsibility, duplicate ownership and the claim that a handoff was complete merely because something was sent.

**M.I.N.D.S.E.T Value Engineering (MVE).** MVE produces a testable hypothesis in a fixed form: if the team takes an action, a named measure should move from a baseline to a target within a timeframe, for a stated reason. It also records the smallest feasible test, the observed result and the decision to adapt, scale, stop or test again. Use it before significant build work begins and wherever value rests on an untested assumption. It replaces premature certainty and a large commitment made before the team knows whether its central belief holds.

**Risk-First Learning Loops (RLL).** RLL produces a risk-first delivery plan and an updated learning record: the highest current TRB exposures, what can be tested this cycle, the smallest safe test, the evidence gained and who needs to know it. Use it at cycle planning and when new information changes risk. It replaces the habit of polishing low-risk work while the uncertainty most able to invalidate the initiative waits until later.

**D.I.R.E.C.T Value Routing (DVR).** DVR produces a recorded choice of the next move, grounded in the TVI, its guardrails, VER, current evidence and confidence. Use it when the team has viable options or when an existing route needs to change. It replaces a decision delayed until a hierarchy meeting, or a choice justified only by preference, because the purpose and decision evidence are visible to the people closest to the work.

**Insight Risk Triggers (IRT).** IRT produces predefined thresholds: the measure or risk to watch, its trigger level and date, the response rule, decision owner and evidence source. Set them before delivery begins and read them on the agreed review cadence. They replace an open-ended instruction to monitor a problem and make clear when the team must pause, pivot, escalate or choose a new test.

**D.I.R.E.C.T Decision Audits (DDA).** A DDA produces a reviewable record of whether a material decision had an explicit purpose, relevant evidence, visible assumptions and risks, fair comparison of options, clear authority, and a documented review point. Use it to log material decisions and audit a sample after a defined period. It replaces reconstructing a decision from meeting memories and judging a decision-maker only by an outcome that may have remained uncertain.

**7 C's Value Engineering Standard (CVE).** CVE produces a quality agreement: value target, quality scale, good-enough threshold, guardrail, cost of the next increment, and a stop-or-continue rule. Use it before development and at review when more polish competes with another priority. It replaces subjective arguments about what “done” means and work that continues beyond the agreed value requirement without an explicit reason.

**7 C's Outcome Metrics (COM).** COM produces a quality scorecard with customer and business measures, supported by technical guardrails, data sources, owners, cadence and decision use. Use it for a released feature or service at the stated review points, including 7, 30 and 60 days where those fit the work. It replaces a technical dashboard used as the only account of quality, such as test coverage, defects or uptime without evidence of customer or business effect.

## Working with TEAM IQ Creator

This book cannot see your teams. It cannot listen to the meeting where a risk is softened, examine a handoff that arrives without the information needed, compare a stated target with the work actually being funded, or tell you which of the six pillars is holding a team back. The instruments give you a way to look, but the diagnosis still depends on real evidence from the people, artefacts, measures and decisions in front of you. A score without that evidence may be a useful prompt; it is not a finding.

Start with the free material. For many teams, it is enough. The TEAM IQ Scorecard on the website takes eight minutes: 24 statements across the six pillars, scored out of 100, with the weakest pillar and two exercises returned. The free monthly workshop takes one pillar at a time in a 60-minute live working session. It includes a practical exercise, questions in the room, printable attendee notes and a replay available for 14 days. The chapters and instruments in this book are also intended for direct use. A team that chooses one real constraint, uses the relevant artefact and reviews the evidence can make a serious start without buying anything.

There are three paid routes when an outside view or more sustained work is useful. The first is the **TEAM IQ Diagnostic Session**, priced at **£1,200 + VAT**. It is a 90-minute session on one area of concern with the team that owns it. It produces a written Team IQ Score, a prioritised action plan and two exercises to start immediately. The £1,200 fee is credited in full against a corporate diagnostic or corporate programme if you continue within six months. The session is a complete piece of work; the plan is written so that a team can use it without further work from TEAM IQ Creator.

The **Corporate TEAM IQ Diagnostic** starts at **£18,000 + VAT** for up to five teams. For six to 12 teams, the stated price is **£32,000 + VAT**; work above 12 teams is scoped before a price is set. It measures all six pillars across every delivery team, benchmarks them on one scale, and produces a prioritised 90-day plan and a board presentation. This route provides the full picture across the teams. It is not presented as the training-and-exercise phase: the price page places training and exercises in the corporate programme, which is a useful distinction to make before anyone commits budget.

The **Corporate TEAM IQ Programme** starts at **£45,000 + VAT**. It runs for 12 to 16 weeks across all six pillars and includes training and exercises for every team, dashboards, coaching for team leads and quarterly re-measurement. The diagnostic fee is credited in full when an organisation proceeds. These are stated starting prices, before VAT. Travel and accommodation for on-site work are charged at cost, and third-party tools or licences are not included unless they are named first.

Work starts with a conversation about what is happening and what evidence already exists. From there, the aim is the smallest useful step: perhaps the free scorecard and an instrument from this book, perhaps one diagnostic session focused on a live constraint, or, where the evidence points to a wider pattern, a corporate diagnostic. There is no need to begin with a programme before the scale of the issue is known.

The standard for this work is the same one used throughout the book. Published findings, the method's own claims and the evidence from a particular team should not be presented as though they are the same thing. A measure should have a named source, owner, baseline, review point and limit. Assumptions should be visible. Where the evidence is incomplete, the honest response is to say so and define the next test, not to claim certainty or promise an outcome.

They are not a benchmark and should not be treated as one.
