## Chapter 3 — The measurement cycle

At 08:30 on a Tuesday, the portfolio office has the numbers ready. There is a delivery dashboard, a risk register, a burn-up chart, a financial forecast and a weekly pack for the executive meeting. Each team has sent its update. Green, amber and red boxes fill the screen.

A director asks a simple question: is the claims team getting better? The room goes quiet. The delivery lead can say how many items moved across the board last week. Finance can say what has been spent. The test lead can say how many defects were found. Nobody can show the change the team set out to create, the starting point, what the latest work was expected to alter, or what they will do because the evidence has changed.

The team is not short of measurement. It is short of a route from measurement to action. The reports describe activity in several places, with different owners and review dates. A problem noticed in a meeting is copied into the next pack, then the next. Work continues because it was planned, not because the latest evidence supports it. By Friday, another report is being prepared. The organisation has measured a great deal and still cannot say whether the team is improving.

### The principle

The **TEAM IQ Measurement Cycle** turns an intended outcome into evidence and then the next decision. Its stages are Set, Communicate, Own, Learn, and Decide and improve. A decision at the end changes the target, work or risk view for the next pass.

Set establishes the valuable change, scale, baseline, target, guardrail, owner and review date. The team and sponsor agree it; the named metric owner maintains it. A shared target statement and T.A.R.G.E.T Value Index (TVI) are the artefacts. Skip Set and later measures may answer different questions.

Communicate makes claims, assumptions, evidence and changes visible. People doing the work bring evidence; the team maintains a common record. The C.L.E.A.R.L.Y Evidence Standard (CES) evidence log and Expression of Value Delta (EVD) update result. Skipped, a repeated opinion can pass as fact.

Own turns a hoped-for result into a commitment with accountability for progress and evidence. The primary owner is responsible; contributors make dependencies explicit. A D.R.E.A.M Outcome Contract (DOC) and, where authority or handoffs need clarity, Quantified D.R.E.A.M Boundaries (QDB), are the artefacts. Skip it and a target belongs to everyone in theory and no one when a trade-off is needed.

Learn tests assumptions that could affect the outcome or invalidate the plan. The outcome owner runs the test with the needed people; the team reviews it. A M.I.N.D.S.E.T Value Engineering (MVE) hypothesis and Risk-First Learning Loops (RLL) record leave the stage, alongside an updated risk view. Skip Learn and commitment rises as uncertainty does.

Decide and improve uses evidence to continue, change, stop or scale work. The named decision owner convenes the decision within the agreed boundary, with the team supplying relevant evidence. A D.I.R.E.C.T Value Routing (DVR) decision, IRT threshold, decision-log entry or DDA is the artefact. Skip this stage and the cycle produces reports that nobody uses; capacity and priorities remain unchanged.

The fourteen TEAM IQ measurement tools are instruments in the loop, not a separate reporting layer. Set uses T.A.R.G.E.T Value Index (TVI), Value-to-Effort Ratio (VER) and Target Risk Burn-Down (TRB). Communicate uses C.L.E.A.R.L.Y Evidence Standard (CES) and Expression of Value Delta (EVD); Own uses D.R.E.A.M Outcome Contract (DOC) and Quantified D.R.E.A.M Boundaries (QDB); Learn uses M.I.N.D.S.E.T Value Engineering (MVE) and Risk-First Learning Loops (RLL). Decide and improve uses D.I.R.E.C.T Value Routing (DVR), Insight Risk Triggers (IRT), D.I.R.E.C.T Decision Audits (DDA), 7 C's Value Engineering Standard (CVE) and 7 C's Outcome Metrics (COM). Each leaves a visible artefact for the next stage, rather than a second report beside the work.

Evidence moves deliberately. The TVI supplies the measure and guardrail for a DOC. CES separates knowledge from what needs testing. MVE and RLL update TRB, EVD and confidence. DVR routes capacity using value, effort, confidence and risk. The decision becomes a changed backlog, test, boundary or target, not a comment in a meeting record.

### What it looks like when it is missing

The team starts the month with a list of commitments inherited from a planning meeting. The list has dates and named task owners, but no shared measure of the customer or business change expected. A dashboard shows completed work, defects and spend. When a senior stakeholder asks whether the work is helping, the team points to the growing list of completed items.

In the weekly meeting, people report that an integration is difficult. Nobody writes the assumption behind the design or decides what evidence would settle it. The risk stays amber because no one has defined a smallest test or named the decision it should inform. The work owner asks three colleagues for approval because the boundary of their authority is unclear.

At month end, the team has a slide describing the difficulty and a request for more time. It has no before-and-after measure, no record of what was learned and no explicit choice to stop, reduce or change the work. The next month begins with the same list, a revised date and another request for an update. Reporting has become the work that proves reporting is happening.

### What it looks like when it works

The same team begins with one TVI: a stated outcome, current baseline, target date and guardrail. It names the person who maintains the measure and the person who owns each material outcome. A short DOC says what evidence will demonstrate progress, what support is needed and what the team will do if the expected movement does not occur.

In the weekly review, the owner uses EVD rather than an activity update. The update shows the before state, current state, learning and next action. One result has not moved. The team records the assumption in CES, puts a small test into the current work and updates the TRB. The difficult integration is now an exposure with a reduction experiment, not a paragraph in a status pack.

At the monthly decision review, the decision owner compares options through DVR. The evidence supports reducing one piece of work, testing another and continuing a third. Those choices are recorded with their rationale and review point. A trigger states what result would cause a pivot. The quarterly review looks across the decisions: whether targets still matter, whether the measures conflict, and whether the team is becoming more able to act on what it learns. The dashboard is shorter because each measure has a decision use.

### How to do it

Run the first pass with one intact team and one material outcome. Do not build a portfolio dashboard or apply every tool. Establish a repeatable loop around work already under way.

1. **Days 1–3: Set the target and initial risk view.** Bring the sponsor, team lead, people closest to customer or service data, and the person who maintains the measure together. Write one TVI with value, scale, baseline, target, guardrail, owner and review date. List work that could move it. Use VER only for items on the same value scale. Create a TRB for the risks most likely to prevent the target, with a smallest risk-reduction experiment. Leave with the target statement, value-ranked work and initial TRB.

2. **Days 3–5: Make evidence and ownership testable.** Start a CES log for claims behind the selected work: supporting evidence, assumption, test, owner and date. Convert the next material commitment into a DOC: outcome, relevant measure, baseline and intended movement, evidence source, timeframe, dependency and learning rule. Use QDB where the owner needs a decision boundary or measurable handoff. Keep these live, not filed.

3. **Weeks 1–3: Run evidence updates and tests.** In the existing weekly meeting, reserve fifteen minutes for EVD. The work owner states what changed, which measure should move, the current observation, learning and next action. Run the MVE test or RLL experiment agreed in week one, favouring the risk with highest exposure. Update CES and TRB as results arrive. If no measure has moved, record learning and the next test rather than inventing progress.

4. **Week 4: route evidence into a decision.** Hold a monthly review with the decision owner and team. Read the TVI, EVD updates, TRB, CES assumptions and MVE or RLL results. Use DVR to compare next moves on value, effort, confidence and risk. Set IRT thresholds for a pause, pivot, escalation or further test. Record the choice, owner, rationale and review date: continue, adapt, stop or scale.

5. **At the quarter boundary: inspect the system, not only the result.** Use DDA on one material decision. Check purpose, evidence, assumptions, risks, options, authority and review point. Where quality is a trade-off, use CVE to set the value target, scale, good-enough threshold, guardrail, cost and stop-or-continue rule. Read COM beside TVI for customer and business impact. Confirm or revise the next target.

Weekly is the rhythm for EVD, CES changes, active DOCs and TRB movement. Monthly is for a DVR decision, IRT review and capacity change. Quarterly is for DDA, target relevance and COM measures. This is a practical starting cadence, not a universal prescription. A fast operational team may need shorter decisions; a slow-moving outcome may not yield useful weekly movement. Keep the review date in the artefact and change cadence deliberately.

### The exercise

Run a 60-minute cycle launch with one team before the first thirty-day pass. Prepare one live outcome that matters now, the best available baseline, the current work list, known risks, and one person able to confirm how the measure is sourced. Bring the sponsor or decision owner if a decision boundary will be needed. Use a shared screen or board that everyone can see.

1. **Set — 15 minutes.** Write the TVI together: value, scale, baseline, target, guardrail, owner and review date. Ask each person to explain the target in their own words. Capture the shared target statement and the one or two risks that most threaten it.
2. **Communicate — 10 minutes.** Choose the claim behind the next item of work. Create one CES entry: claim, available evidence, assumption, experiment, owner and learning needed. Capture the evidence source and date rather than a vague confidence statement.
3. **Own — 10 minutes.** Turn that item into a DOC. Name the outcome, measure, evidence source, timeframe, dependencies and learning rule. Confirm the primary owner and, if needed, the QDB decision boundary. Capture unresolved support needs separately.
4. **Learn — 10 minutes.** Design the smallest MVE or RLL test that could reduce the most consequential uncertainty. State what observation would change the team’s view. Add it to the current work and capture the planned test date.
5. **Decide and improve — 10 minutes.** Agree the first decision point, decision owner and IRT threshold. Write the future DVR question: continue, change, stop or scale what? Capture where the EVD, TRB and decision log will be read.
6. **Close — 5 minutes.** Schedule the weekly evidence review and monthly decision review. Photograph or save the board. The launch does not replace the test; it makes the first test and the decision it should inform visible.

### What to measure

The first measure is the TVI: a current baseline, scale, target, guardrail, named owner and review date for the outcome. Its artefact is a target card or shared record that the team reads weekly. It prevents a pile of delivery measures from standing in for an outcome.

Read EVD and the CES log weekly to see whether the evidence behind current work is changing. Read the TRB with them when risk is material. The monthly artefact is the DVR decision record, supported by IRT thresholds and the decision log: what the team continued, changed, stopped or scaled, why, and when it will be reviewed. At quarter end, use DDA and COM to test whether the decision process and outcome measures remain connected to value, quality and risk.

### One thing to do this week

Choose one team and one decision due in the next month. Before its next planning meeting, write a one-page TVI: the outcome, baseline, target, guardrail, owner and review date. Ask every attendee to explain it without reading the page. Where their accounts differ, do not move to the work list yet. Resolve the target first, then schedule a fifteen-minute weekly EVD review and name the decision owner for the month-end choice.
