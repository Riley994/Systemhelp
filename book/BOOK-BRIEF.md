# TEAM IQ — the book: writer's brief

Read this before writing a single word. It is the contract for every chapter.

---

## The book

**Title:** Team IQ: why your best people don't make your best teams
**Subtitle:** The six pillars of predictable high performance
**Author:** Andrew Phillips
**Publisher:** Network Advansys Limited, trading as Systemhelp
**Reader:** delivery directors, heads of transformation, PMO leads and COOs in organisations
running more than one team. They have met Agile and are past being impressed by it. They have
real budget pressure and a board that wants outcomes, not ceremonies.

**Promise to the reader:** by the end, you can measure the collective intelligence of a team,
say what is holding it back, raise it pillar by pillar, and show the movement to someone who
holds the money.

## The voice

Plain, direct, and specific. The reader is competent and short of time. Write like a
practitioner who has run programmes explaining something to a peer, not like a consultant
presenting a framework.

- **British English throughout.** organisation, recognise, behaviour, programme, prioritise,
  analyse, centre, whilst → while, -ise not -ize.
- **Sentences that vary in length.** Mostly short. One long sentence occasionally is fine.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the artefact, the meeting, the number, the document. "The
  weekly status report that nobody reads" beats "communication challenges".
- **Second person for the reader** (you, your teams, your programme). First person singular
  only where it is true of Andrew, from the cv facts in `source/evidence-and-sources.md`
  and the list below. First person plural ("we") means Systemhelp, used sparingly.
- **No hype.** No exclamation marks. No rhetorical question stacks. No "imagine if".
- **Honest about what is not proven.** Where a claim is ours rather than a published finding,
  say so in the sentence. This honesty is the book's differentiator and must not be softened.

### Words that are banned outright

solutions, empower, unlock, leverage, journey, seamless, robust, holistic, cutting-edge,
world-class, best-in-class, game-changing, paradigm, ecosystem, delve, testament to, tapestry,
"it's not about X, it's about Y", "at the end of the day", "in today's fast-paced world",
"we are passionate about", and any sentence beginning "It is important to note that".

If a banned word appears in the source material, rewrite around it. "Synergy" may appear once,
in the chapter whose subject it is, defined plainly in the same sentence.

## The evidence rules — the hard limit

Every statistic, study, client name and quotation must come from one of two files:

1. `source/evidence-and-sources.md` — the published research and the sourced figures.
2. The cv facts, listed below, which are already published on the website.

**Never invent a statistic, a study, an author, a client, a quotation or a case study.** Not one,
not as an illustration. If a point needs a number you do not have, make the point without a
number.

Where you cite research, name it in the prose as a reader would recognise it — Google's Project
Aristotle, the MIT collective intelligence studies, and so on — and keep it to one or two
sentences. The reference list is assembled separately; you do not need to build it.

## Chapter anatomy — follow this shape exactly

Every chapter has these sections, in this order, with these `###` headings. Word targets are
per chapter; keep within 15% either way.

| Section | Words | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *(opening, no heading)* | 150–250 | One concrete situation from delivery life. No definition, no quote, no statistics. Make the reader recognise the room. |
| `### The principle` | 400–500 | What this is, why it matters, what it costs when it is absent. The evidence if there is any. |
| `### What it looks like when it is missing` | 200–260 | A short, concrete contrast. Specific behaviours, not adjectives. |
| `### What it looks like when it works` | 200–260 | The same situation with the pillar in place, equally concrete, not aspirational. |
| `### How to do it` | 500–650 | The practical method. Numbered steps where it is a procedure. Name the TEAM IQ tools and metrics exactly as the course source names them. |
| `### The exercise` | 250–350 | The course's exercise for this chapter, written so a manager can run it in a 45–60 minute session: what to prepare, how to run it, what to capture, how long each part takes. |
| `### What to measure` | 120–180 | The metric or tool this chapter produces, what the artefact is, and how often it is read. |
| `### One thing to do this week` | 60–110 | A single action, small enough to actually happen. |

Total per chapter: 1,900–2,150 words.

### Markdown conventions

- Chapter heading: `## Chapter 4 — True and explainable` (H2). Do **not** use H1 in a chapter file;
  H1 belongs to the parts, which are assembled separately.
- Section headings: `### Exact heading from the table above`.
- No images, no code blocks, no blockquotes.
- Pipe tables are allowed where a list of pairs genuinely reads better as a table.
- Bold sparingly — for a term's first definition, not for emphasis.
- Italic for book and report titles and for a word being defined.
- Use `-` for bullets and `1.` `2.` for steps. One level of nesting maximum.
