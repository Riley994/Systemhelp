TEAM IQ Benchmark

A practical comparison point for organisations measuring how their teams think, decide, learn and deliver together.

Important: an early, small-sample benchmark

Based on the first 23 organisational assessments — treat as directional, not definitive. This is not a large-scale study or a population norm. It is a deliberately cautious snapshot to support a better conversation about team capability, not a league table or prediction.

What the benchmark is

Each organisational assessment brings together the six TEAM IQ pillars: TARGET, EXPRESSION, ACCOUNTABILITY, MINDSET, INSIGHT and QUALITY. Scores help an organisation identify where its operating system enables collective performance and where it creates friction.

Most common weakest pillar

EXPRESSION

In this early cohort, EXPRESSION most often appeared as the lowest pillar: teams lacked a reliable shared route for challenge, evidence, decisions and learning to travel.

What this does not mean

Not a people problem

A low pillar score is a signal to examine the conditions, rituals and artefacts around the team—not a judgement about commitment or talent.

How to use the comparison

Read the pattern · Not the rank

What the early cohort suggests

Common patterns

  • Activity was clearer than a measurable stakeholder outcome or baseline.
  • Decisions sat in meetings or chat rather than a visible route.
  • Task ownership was clearer than cross-team handoffs and learning experiments.

What separates higher scores

  • A target people can explain and measure without translation.
  • Safety combined with an evidence standard—not comfort without challenge.
  • Outcome owners, measurable handoffs, decision records and learning loops.

How to read your own score

Use it as a starting hypothesis, then test your pillar pattern against target cards, decision logs, handoff conditions, customer measures and team experience. A score is not a rank; it is a prompt for the next useful intervention.

Typical improvement over 12–16 weeks

Observed range across the first cohort—not a promise. Movement depended on baseline, support, stability and use in live work.

Why measure the team, not only the individual?

The published work cited in the TEAM IQ course converges: MIT links collective intelligence with social sensitivity and balanced participation; Google identifies psychological safety, dependability, structure and clarity as team dynamics; SAGE 2024 shows wider observed variation in team than individual performance; and InnerCircle and Scrum Inc indicate that process change alone can fail. Measure the conditions in which people work together—not people in isolation.
